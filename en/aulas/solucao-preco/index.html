Lesson 3 · 18 min

Solution and a price that scales

The client can't judge whether your technical solution is good: they've never rolled out AI. What they judge is whether you look like you know what you're doing, and they read that in your method. This lesson shows the method (survey, measure, pilot, scale) and the pricing in steps.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1Map the current state in 4 spreadsheets
  2. 2Freeze the baseline before you touch anything
  3. 3Build the pricing ladder and the service menu
  4. 4Write the one-page proposal

Goal: map the current state, measure the baseline, and put together the one-page proposal with 3 options in steps.

01 · The method that builds trust

Trust grows with each step delivered on time, before any technical result.

Did adoption drop in the third week? The process didn't fit the routine: go back to the mapping.
Click each block in the diagram to see what it does.

Models get discontinued. Monitor what you delivered in a monthly advisory, or give the client the list of what's in use and what to do when it changes.

02 · The pricing ladder

Dollar ranges as an order of magnitude for the international market. Adapt to your market.

1. Access

A shared space where you answer questions once a week.

It holds the client who had one or two conversations and would disappear without it.

2. Single call

Always with prep work beforehand: you show up knowing the industry and the likely stack.

Bundle it when it makes sense: one session plus five questions by email in the month.

3. Monthly advisory

Weekly meeting, email support in an agreed format, team training, and priority on your calendar.

This is the step that turns consulting into predictable work.

4. Project

Several departments, a long timeline, and specialists subcontracted for what isn't yours.

Here you answer for the result. The build, when there is one, is just one part of it.

RuleWrite the steps on a single page, for yourself. Improvising a price in the middle of the conversation is how the discount is born.

03 · The service menu and the 60-minute call

Let the client build the plate instead of choosing between packages they don't understand.

1. Service menu

Starters (beginner), quick dishes (practical wins), intermediate, advanced, desserts (executive level) and banquets (closed packages).

Each item has just four fields: name, duration (90 to 150 min), level and a short description of the result, never of the tool.

EffectThe client stops asking "how much does it cost" and starts saying "I want number 3, number 4 and the light version of number 8."

2. 0 to 15 min

Start with the pain, in their own words, and close with the goal repeated out loud.

Then: scale, stack and where the data lives.

3. 15 to 35 min

Play back what you understood and present 2 or 3 hypotheses, stated as hypotheses.

In the recommendation, say why it may not be realistic for their business and show cheaper or faster alternatives.

4. 35 to 40 min

Offer the steps and leave with one task: list the tools with the number of users, choose the pilot department or name an internal owner.

The twenty minutes left are for the question they saved for the end.

04 · Templates to copy

The proposal fits on one page. The baseline comes before any target number.

One-page proposal
PROPOSAL · <company> · <date>

1. WHAT WE HEARD
<the pain in the client's own words, in 2 lines>

2. BASELINE (measured on <date>, by <how it was measured>)
- <indicator>: <current value> → target <value> in <timeframe>

3. THREE WAYS TO WORK TOGETHER
A) Access · <amount/month>
   Weekly Q&A group. For those who want to start on their own with support.
B) Pilot · <amount> · <30 to 60 days>
   A group of <n> people in <area>, one metric, report at the end.
C) Project · <amount> · <timeframe>
   Pilot + scale-up to <department> + <n> months of follow-up.

4. WHAT IS NOT INCLUDED
<e.g.: integration with system X; running campaigns>

5. RISK AND TRADE-OFF
<what may not work and what the company gives up with this choice>

6. NEXT STEP
<client's task> by <date>. Next conversation: <date>.
Baseline · one indicator
Indicator: <e.g.: time to answer a quote request>
Current value: <number and unit>
Target: <number> in <timeframe>
How it is measured: <who measures it, where the number comes from, how it is counted>
Review frequency: <weekly or monthly; never daily>
Prompt · generate your service menu
You are a senior AI consultant building the service menu for your own
business. Write in Brazilian Portuguese, direct tone, no sales adjectives.

My profile: <what you can really do today>
My clients: <industry, size and job title of the people who hire you>
What I do NOT do: <list it, so it stays off the menu>

Build a menu with 6 categories: starters (beginner), quick dishes
(practical wins), intermediate, advanced, desserts (executive level) and
banquets (closed packages). At least 30 items in total.
For EACH item, exactly 4 fields:
- name (short, no jargon)
- delivery duration, between 90 and 150 minutes
- level (beginner / intermediate / advanced / executive)
- description in at most 2 lines, stating the result, not the tool
Prioritize diagnosis, teaching, review and follow-up; building only
as part of one of these.
End with: "Menu updated in <month and year>".

05 · Practice

Use a real client or the clinic case from lesson 2.

1 · Baseline of a process

20 min

Pick one of the client's processes (or the simulated clinic's) and fill in the baseline template for 2 indicators.

Done when: both indicators have a current value as a number, a target with a deadline, and the way of measuring written down.

2 · One-page proposal

40 min

Fill in the proposal template with your baseline and the 3 options in steps. Write down what is not included and the trade-off of the recommended option.

Done when: the proposal fits on one page, has the baseline as a number and 3 options (access, pilot, project) with price and timeframe.
